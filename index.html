Platform problem

People willing to help and people needing help often fail to meet.

Chain of Happiness treats this as a coordination problem. Needs and available resources are represented explicitly so the platform can support matching across financial and non-financial forms of contribution.

Research problem

Participation changes the system that participants experience.

More users can create more potential matches and visibility, but growth can also create congestion, unmatched requests, trust problems, or declining response quality. Those feedbacks are central to the System Dynamics research.

Product logic

Core platform design

Requests & offers

Represent needs and available resources as distinct objects that can be coordinated.

Non-monetary help

Support time, skills, services, goods, knowledge, and other resources—not only cash.

Integrity rules

Rules such as preventing users from funding their own requests are intended to protect trust in the exchange process.

Matching

Reduce friction between available resources and expressed needs.

Research-informed iteration

Treat incentives, matching logic, participation, and trust mechanisms as hypotheses that can be examined.

Scalable coordination

Explore how repeated successful interactions can support a larger social-support system.

Illustrative feedback

Why System Dynamics?

successful help→trust & visibility→participation→potential matches→successful help

That reinforcing loop is only one side of the system. Balancing feedback can arise from congestion, low-quality matching, delays, or loss of trust. Modeling makes those competing mechanisms explicit.
